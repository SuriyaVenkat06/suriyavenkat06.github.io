Resource Efficient Products, TKMJ29 · Linköping University

Multi-method sustainability analysis of a coffee machine

Group study running a full ecodesign toolkit — Design for Disassembly, Life Cycle Assessment, an Ecodesign Checklist, QFDE and Ashby material selection — on a commercial coffee machine to find where its design worked against recyclability and environmental performance.

-56.8%
Disassembly loss
98.4%
Impact from use phase

DfD redesigns (snap-fit joints, standardised fasteners, slide-locks) cut the disassembly loss from −440.85 to −190.45 SEK. The LCA (functional unit: 10,000 cups over 2 years) found eutrophication as the dominant impact category and the use phase — electricity and coffee production — as the largest contributor at 98.4% of total impact. QFDE-ranked redesigns were scored for environmental improvement, with the top concept scoring 20.75 vs. 14.19 for the alternative, and Ashby material charts were used to screen replacement materials on price against embodied energy.

Industrial Energy Systems, TMES21 · Linköping University

Energy audit and efficiency roadmap for a Boxholm steel plant

Group energy audit of a cold-drawn steel bar manufacturer in Boxholm, Östergötland (~50,000 t/year output), combining top-down and bottom-up analysis of electricity and oil use across production machinery, lighting, compressed air, ventilation and the boiler to build a full energy balance and a prioritised efficiency roadmap.

1,474 MWh
Top measure: smart scheduling
0.4 yr
Payback on that measure

Mapped 3,794.8 MWh of purchased electricity and 1,702 MWh of boiler energy against individual processes — bulk-throwing machines, drawing benches and compressed air among the largest consumers — then costed six efficiency measures. Smart equipment scheduling and LED relighting topped the priority list on payback (0.4 and 1.2 years); compressed-air leak fixes and pressure optimisation followed, with boiler upgrades and solar panels ranked as longer-term investments (6.3 and 40-year payback).
